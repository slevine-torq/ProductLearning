# Module 2 · Glossary: Discover Product Opportunities via Qualitative Signals

> Key terms from Module 2, in plain language. Bookmark this for the rest of the certification.

---

**Qualitative signals**: Non-numerical evidence of user experience: interview quotes, support tickets, bug reports, reviews, and observed behavior. The raw material of discovery.

**Co-pilot / pilot**: The modern discovery partnership. **AI (the co-pilot)** scales the heavy lifting by synthesizing thousands of data points into themes instantly. **The PM (the pilot)** owns the strategic edge, curating inputs and applying empathy, domain knowledge, and judgment to decide what to build.

**Traditional vs. AI-powered discovery**: *Traditional* discovery is slow and batch-based, with long cycles between insight and action; the PM spends most time on "what did they say?" *AI-powered* discovery is fast and iterative, raw data to synthesized insight in minutes, freeing the PM to ask "what does it mean?"

**Core values as a discovery filter**: Using company values as the lens for every discovery effort: ensuring **strategic alignment**, acting as the **problem filter** (loud complaint vs. problem worth resources), and setting the **success criteria** for what a win looks like.

**Discovery methods**: The three buckets of research: **Foundational** (explore needs before building), **Evaluative** (test designs and prototypes), and **Strategic** (measure, prioritize, and validate).

**Attitudinal vs. behavioral**: *Attitudinal* methods capture what users **say** (interviews, surveys, focus groups). *Behavioral* methods capture what users **do** (contextual inquiry, session recordings, analytics, A/B tests). Behavior is the more reliable signal of real friction.

**Contextual inquiry**: A behavioral, foundational method: observing users in their real environment. Uncovers invisible workarounds a feature request would never reveal.

**Continuous discovery**: Weekly, lightweight customer interviews woven into the product cycle (the Teresa Torres method), as opposed to large, infrequent research batches.

**User persona**: A *behavioral anchor* for product decisions, not a demographic bio. Built from shared goals and the workarounds users adopt to bypass friction. (Example: LinkedIn Learning's "Completionist" vs. "Explorer.")

**The "average user" trap**: Defining a persona by demographics (age, location) instead of behavior, producing a vague user nobody is actually designing for.

**Behaviors / needs / pain points**: The three categories of discovery insight. **Behaviors (actions)**: what users actually do. **Needs (goals)**: the functional or emotional job they're trying to get done. **Pain points (friction)**: the repetitive, manual moments of misery.

**Moment of misery**: The specific point where a product fails a user, forcing a manual workaround. The human anchor of an opportunity.

**Good vs. bad discovery questions**: *Good* questions anchor users in past or present reality ("Talk me through the last time…", "How are you dealing with this now?"). *Bad* questions are future-tense hypotheticals ("Would you buy…?", "How much would you pay…?") that invite social-desirability bias.

**Journey map**: A visual representation of a user's experience over time, tracking what they do, think, and feel at every touchpoint. Its value is exposing **experience gaps**.

**Experience gap**: A specific stage in the journey where users get stuck, drop off, or feel frustration, the emotional low point that points to the next product opportunity.

**Touchpoint**: Any place a user interacts with your product, support, or related tools, including external apps where friction often hides (e.g. switching to WhatsApp to share).

**Current state vs. future state**: A journey map of how things work *today* (with all the manual hacks) vs. an *idealized* workflow your initiative will deliver.

**The status quo**: The user's existing manual habits and external tools. Your real competitor, because most users default to "no decision" rather than the risk of switching.

**"No decision"**: When a user decides their current "good enough" process is safer than trying something new. The most common way a product loses.

**The breaking point**: The moment where the cumulative cost of inaction finally outweighs the pain and effort of switching.

**The strategic gap**: The specific way current solutions fail, so you can build something that renders the user's manual hacks and external workarounds obsolete.

**Competitive intelligence (CI)**: The practice of identifying competitors, creating comparison assets, and extracting intel from sources, fundamentals now supercharged by AI, shifting the PM from "researcher" to "orchestrator."

**ICP (Ideal Customer Profile)**: The specific customer a product is built for; the input that lets AI generate a tiered, rationale-backed competitor list in minutes.

**Product Health & Insights Summary**: The Exercise 1 deliverable: an executive-level synthesis of UXR notes and bug reports, grouped into themes with severity, deliberately excluding a roadmap.

**Logic leak**: A place where the AI oversteps the PM's judgment, inventing a roadmap, suggesting features, or hallucinating beyond the data. The thing your audit is built to catch.

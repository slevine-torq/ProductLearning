Section 01
Strategic Discovery & Problem Validation
AI can summarise a thousand interviews in seconds. But synthesis isn’t strategy, your value is the discernment to decide which problems are actually worth solving.
Instructor-Led Q&A · 10 min

Challenges with product discovery

Unmute and share, drop it in chat, or post in #cohort-channel.

💬
Now that AI can help narrow down UXR, analytics, and other research, what are the main challenges you see PMs having when it comes to actually understanding user problems?

The takeaway

AI provides the synthesis; the PM provides the discernment. Your value is in grounding insights in strategy so you’re solving a problem that actually moves the needle, not just the loudest complaint in the dataset.

The modern PM partnership

The evolution of discovery

To be a modern PM you must shift how you discover, moving from manual reporting to strategic synthesis. AI handles the heavy lifting so your energy goes to the strategic question.

🕰️ Old way · traditional discovery
“What did they say?”
Slow, high-effort, done in large infrequent batches
Long cycles between an insight and any action
PM time spent on “What did the users say?”
Manual synthesis: reading every ticket and transcript
➔
⚡ New way · AI-powered discovery
“What does it mean?”
Fast, iterative, raw data to synthesized insight in minutes
Fosters a culture of continuous discovery
PM time spent on “What does this mean for our product?”
AI aggregates; you decide what matters

Co-pilot vs. pilot

AI (the co-pilot) scales the heavy lifting, synthesizing thousands of data points and mapping evidence into clear themes instantly. You (the pilot) own the strategic edge: curating high-quality inputs and applying empathy, domain knowledge, and judgment to decide what to build.

Core values as a discovery filter

Discovery starts with your company’s soul

Discovery doesn’t start with a user interview, it starts with knowing what your company stands for. Without that filter you’ll find thousands of problems and have no idea which ones to ignore.

Strategic Alignment

Values ensure the discovery effort moves the company toward its long-term destination, not just a short-term fix. Insights must be strategically relevant, not merely interesting.

The Problem Filter

They establish the non-negotiables that distinguish a loud user complaint from a strategic problem worth company resources, your professional permission to ignore the noise.

Success Criteria

They set the benchmark for a “win”, whether you’re optimizing for market share, profitability, or customer delight, so you look for the right opportunity from the start.

Filter to what moves the needle

Core values taper your focus from the big picture down to specific outcomes, ensuring the problems you choose to solve are the ones that actually matter for the business.

Discovery methods

Choose the right evidence for the question

Three buckets, Foundational, Evaluative, Strategic. Balance attitudinal (what users say) with behavioral (what users do): rely on behavior to see what users actually do when faced with friction.

🔭
Foundational
Explore needs before building
SAY
User Interviews
1:1 conversations to uncover motivations, mental models, and pain points.
SAY
Focus Groups
Group discussions to surface shared attitudes and early concept feedback.
DO
Contextual Inquiry
Observe users in their real environment. Uncovers invisible workarounds.
DO
Diary Studies
Participants log experiences over days/weeks. Great for longitudinal insight.
🧪
Evaluative
Test designs & prototypes
DO
Usability Testing
Watch users attempt tasks. Identifies friction, confusion, and failure points.
DO
Unmoderated Testing
Remote async tasks via Maze / UserTesting. Fast, scalable, high volume.
DO
Session Recordings
Replay real sessions (Hotjar, FullStory). Find rage clicks and drop-offs.
DO
A/B Testing
Compare variants at scale. Strong causal signal, tells you what, not why.
📊
Strategic
Measure, prioritize & validate
SAY
Surveys
Structured questions at scale. Measure satisfaction (NPS, CSAT) and segment users.
DO
Continuous Discovery
Weekly lightweight interviews in the product cycle (Teresa Torres).
DO
Product Analytics
Funnels, retention, adoption. Quantitative behavior data at scale.
DO
Card Sorting / Tree Test
Validate information architecture and how users mentally group content.

The gold is often behavioral

Early Slack PMs shadowed users and saw them tracking tasks in spreadsheets to escape the chat UI, a workaround no feature request ever mentioned. Behavioral evidence reveals the problems attitudinal methods miss.

Designing for specificity

User personas: behavioral anchors, not bios

Define a persona by age or location and you fall into the “average user” trap. Advanced discovery maps the ecosystem by shared goals and the workarounds users build to bypass friction. LinkedIn Learning found two behaviors that demanded different product decisions:

Persona 1 · driven by progress
The Completionist
Driven by structured paths, linear progress, and formal recognition
Aligns with deep engagement and long-term retention
Product decision: course-completion badges and granular progress tracking to reward the need for a finish line.
Persona 2 · driven by flexibility
The Explorer
Driven by flexibility and quick answers to a specific problem
Optimizes for efficiency over completion
Product decision: customizable playlists and modular lessons so they skip the linear fluff and reach the “Aha!” fast.

Prioritize via intentional trade-offs

You’ve already filtered the problem through your core values, now decide which behavior to reward first based on pain severity and business impact, without becoming an everything-for-everyone mess.

Synthesis

Three core categories of discovery insights

Collecting data is half the battle, your value is in synthesis. Group raw feedback into three buckets so it becomes actionable.

Actions
Behaviors
What users actually do, not what they say. Watch for drop-offs, repeat clicks, and the workarounds they’ve built.
Users returned daily but churned when progress plateaued. This behavior insight led to gamified Streaks that sustain a daily habit.
Goals
Needs
The functional or emotional “job” the user is trying to get done, the ultimate outcome they’re chasing.
Teams were hacking shared folders to collaborate. This goal-oriented insight led to Dropbox for Business.
Friction
Pain Points
The “moments of misery”, the repetitive, manual tasks that make users sigh with frustration.
Users manually recreated the same sizes repeatedly. This friction insight led to pre-set formats and Magic Resize: publish 10× faster.

Journey mapping

From pain point to solution

A journey map is a visual representation of a user’s experience over time, what they do, think, and feel at every touchpoint. Its value: it reveals the experience gaps where users get stuck or drop off, giving you a clear target for what to improve next.

1
🎯
Set the goal
Establish the specific start and end points of the task you’re analyzing.
2
🧍
Choose a persona
Focus on one specific user type to keep the journey narrow and accurate.
3
📐
Define the scope
Decide if you’re mapping the current state or a future, idealized experience.
4
🪜
Outline the steps
List the sequential actions, and what the user is doing, thinking, and feeling.
5
📍
Add touchpoints
Identify every place the user interacts with your product or support team.
6
⚡
Spot key moments
Pinpoint the stages of high frustration or delight, your experience gaps.

Map the emotion, not just the steps

Assess the finished map through the lens of your research. The emotional highs and lows, driven by a poor interface, a confusing flow, or a moment of value, shouldn’t just be observed; they should guide your next product decision.

Real-world example

Spotify’s music-sharing journey map

Spotify mapped the journey from opening the app to a friend’s reply across seven phases. The emotional curve exposes exactly where the experience breaks down.

The dip is the opportunity

At Receive, users hit a low point, “Why must I open it in Spotify? That’s annoying.” That emotional dip is the experience gap. By fixing the Share → Receive flow, Spotify turned frustrated moments into happy ones. The journey map shows you precisely where the experience is failing.

Market & Competitive Analysis
Your most dangerous competitor isn’t a rival app, it’s the user who decides their messy spreadsheet is safer than the risk of trying something new.
The status quo

Your #1 competitor is the status quo

The biggest threat to your product isn’t a rival, it’s “no decision.” Users stick with familiar friction because change feels riskier than staying the same. To move them, your discovery must prove two things:

1 · The breaking point
Make inaction the liability
Solve a problem so critical that the cumulative cost of “doing nothing” finally outweighs the pain of switching
A team tolerates slow manual data entry for months, until it causes a breach or a quarterly revenue hit. Pinpoint that exact threshold.
2 · The strategic gap
Render the workaround obsolete
Provide a solution that doesn’t just add features but explicitly makes the user’s manual hacks and external workarounds unnecessary
A user exports to Excel, runs three macros, then uploads elsewhere for a report. That spreadsheet is your real competitor.

Stop guessing features

Identify the breaking point and the strategic gap and you stop guessing what to build, you start solving the actual friction keeping users stuck.

Competitive intelligence

Three best practices, now supercharged with AI

The fundamentals of CI haven’t changed, but your time to insight has. The shift moves the PM from “researcher” to “orchestrator,” acting on market changes in real time.

1
Identify competitors
🕒 Old · manualManual Google searches, analyst reports, asking Sales. Slow and prone to blind spots.
➔
⚡ New · AI-poweredPrompt AI with your ICP, pricing tier, and use case, get a tiered competitor list with rationale in minutes.
2
Create CI assets
🕒 Old · manualWrite one-pagers and comparison docs from scratch. Takes weeks, goes stale fast.
➔
⚡ New · AI-poweredFeed AI a competitor’s site and G2 reviews, get a structured comparison doc ready to edit, not write.
3
Get intel from sources
🕒 Old · manualRead help docs and press releases manually. Hard to scale across competitors.
➔
⚡ New · AI-poweredSet AI agents to alert you when competitors change pricing, ship features, or get new reviews.

From writing to editing

AI drafts the competitor list, the comparison doc, and the monitoring, your job shifts to refining and deciding, not collecting.

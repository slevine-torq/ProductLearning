Why this module matters

In Module 1 you framed the problem; in Module 2 you discovered it qualitatively. Module 3 is where your hunch meets the hard numbers. The goal isn't to drown in dashboards, it's to choose the one metric that proves a user's life actually got better, find where the friction lives, size how urgent it is, and turn all of it into a measurable bet you can defend to engineering.

The through-line: data is cheap; the right metric, and the right question, is expensive.

The three things to anchor on

  1. A metric is only useful if you can move it. Filter every candidate through three pillars, clarify the goal, focus on control, and maintain balance (watch a counter-metric). Then dodge the three traps: false signals (vanity clicks), lack of context (the why lives in your research), and averages (which hide segment-level friction). Chase a leading indicator you control, not a lagging result you can only mourn.
  2. Slice, then size. A single number tells you what is happening. Segmentation slices it to find where the pain is (behavior, tenure, environment, account tier). Cohort analysis then sizes it over time: a decline is a leak (needs a fix); a flatline is a ceiling (needs an improvement).
  3. A hypothesis is a measurable contract. Turn your slices and sizes into a bet using the decision formula, discovery evidence → problem → persona → strategic outcome → success metric, and protect it with a guardrail metric and a decision window.

Key terms (full list in the Glossary)

  • Vanity vs. strategic metric: counting an action (clicks, dollars moved) vs. proving an outcome (the user succeeded).
  • Leading indicator vs. lagging result: an early signal you can influence vs. an outcome you can only observe after the fact.
  • AARRR: Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Revenue, Referral; the lifecycle funnel that pinpoints where to act.
  • Segmentation: slicing a metric by a trait to find where the friction is.
  • Cohort analysis: tracking groups who started at the same time to size a problem over its lifetime.
  • Guardrail metric: the number that must not drop; it protects your existing base while you chase the win.

Come ready with

  • A reaction to our opening Quick Vote (vanity vs. strategic metric), be ready to defend your reasoning.
  • Your Module 1 Problem Hook and your Module 2 persona, friction point, and workaround: the lab reconciles them against real data.
  • Your AI account (Gemini, ChatGPT, or Claude), active and logged in before class, the instructor won't pause to troubleshoot logins.

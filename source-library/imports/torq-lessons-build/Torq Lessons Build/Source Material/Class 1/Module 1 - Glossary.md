# Module 1 · Glossary: Develop Strategic Product Thinking

> Key terms from Module 1, in plain language. Bookmark this for the rest of the certification.

---

**Product Manager (PM)**: The person who sits at the center of a cross-functional team and is accountable for *what* gets built and *why*. Not the boss of any function, the strategic arbiter who turns ambiguity into a clear, defensible direction.

**Feature factory**: An output-driven way of working where success is measured by how many features shipped and whether deadlines were hit. The opposite of outcome-driven product management.

**Value driver**: The modern PM mode: a strategic architect who measures success by business impact and behavior change, not by the release date.

**The three pillars of prioritization**: The three lenses every request is run through: **customer needs** (does it solve a real frustration?), **business goals** (does it move a metric that matters?), and **feasibility / resources** (can we build it well?). The highest-impact work sits where all three overlap.

**North Star**: A single shared metric or direction that tells the team what to optimize for. Without it, "everything is urgent" and nothing is a priority.

**Outcome vs. output**: *Output* is what you ship (features, releases). *Outcome* is the measurable change in user behavior or business result that shipping was supposed to produce.

**The PM skill stack**: The five capabilities a modern PM must be fluent in: **strategic thinking, communication, data literacy, customer & market insights, and AI fluency**.

**Strategic arbiter**: The PM's core role: deciding which direction is worth the team's effort and connecting people, ideas, and goals so the pieces cohere.

**B2C (Business-to-Consumer)**: Products serving large consumer audiences (e.g. Spotify, Netflix). PM value comes from scale: fast experimentation, optimizing for emotion and habit. You filter *data noise*.

**B2B (Business-to-Business)**: Products serving organizations and high-value clients (e.g. Snowflake, Atlassian, Salesforce). PM value comes from ROI and workflow efficiency through long sales cycles and deep discovery. The user often isn't the buyer. You filter *stakeholder noise*.

**JTBD, Jobs to Be Done**: A lens that asks what *job* a customer is "hiring" a product to do, rather than what features they ask for. The foundation of strategic discovery.

**PDLC, Product Development Life Cycle**: The industry-standard map for taking an idea from a hunch to a finished product: **Discovery → Research → Design → Development → Testing → Launch → Iteration**. A series of strategic, repeatable decisions.

**Discovery**: The first PDLC phase: gathering insights from market trends, user pain, and business strategy to find a promising opportunity. The PM synthesizes messy data into a structured problem statement.

**Research**: The second PDLC phase: validating an opportunity through data and interviews so the team builds something the market actually needs, and defining success metrics before work begins.

**Design / Development / Testing**: The execution phases of the PDLC, where the validated *why* becomes a functional *what* and the PM acts as the "hub," balancing constraints and protecting quality.

**Launch / Iteration**: The final PDLC phases: orchestrating the market release and then using real user behavior to decide whether to double down, pivot, or move on. Shipping is the beginning of a new data loop, not the end.

**AI-powered life cycle**: Using AI to move through the PDLC faster: AI synthesizes inputs, drafts PRDs and prototypes, and generates launch materials, while the PM keeps the high-value decisions.

**Problem hook**: A single, urgent sentence that fuses the business risk with the user pain to explain why an initiative must be solved now. The opening of the final project.

**Value proposition**: A statement of who a solution is for, the new value it provides, and why it's urgent to launch now.

**Strategic crisis**: The high-level business risk in a scenario: what the company stands to lose if the status quo continues for 12 months.

**Moment of misery**: The specific point where a product fails a user, forcing a manual workaround (Google, spreadsheets, a competitor), the human anchor of a problem hook.

**Discovery map**: A structured product mindmap (e.g. built in Figma) that organizes opportunities around jobs-to-be-done, effort vs. impact, monetization, and retention, distinct from a generic, AI-generated feature list.

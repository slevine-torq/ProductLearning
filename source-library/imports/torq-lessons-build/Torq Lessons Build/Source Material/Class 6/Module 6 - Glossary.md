# Module 6 · Glossary: Launch Impactful GTM Plans

Quick reference for the terms used in Module 6. Keep this open during the lab.

## Core concepts

- **GTM (go-to-market) plan**: the PM's strategic output for a launch. It answers three questions: *who needs to know about this, how do they find out, and how do we know it worked?* It runs in parallel with the build, not after it.
- **The 7-part launch checklist**: goal & audience → launch tier → channel plan → enablement & assets → budget & resources → timeline → success metrics.
- **Launch vs. release**: a **release** is when the product is technically ready for customer use; a **launch** is when you activate go-to-market efforts around it. They don't need to coincide, and some capabilities can simply be released without a launch.
- **GTM as a multiplier**: go-to-market amplifies a product that already has product-market fit; it cannot create fit that isn't there.

## Goal & audience

- **Awareness goal**: make the market aware the product exists and how it should be perceived. Audience is usually external (new prospects, adjacent users, new segments).
- **Engagement goal**: drive meaningful interaction among existing users (adoption, feedback, loyalty). Audience lives inside your current user base.
- **Conversion goal**: connect the launch to a measurable business outcome (acquire, upsell, retain). Audience is precise, people ready to move to the next stage.
- **The goal determines the audience**: not the other way around. A conversion goal without a defined segment is wishful thinking.

## Launch tier (t-shirt sizing)

- **S, Minimal**: no proactive outreach; the feature ships and the right users find it.
- **M, Targeted**: limited channels, focused comms (e.g. email + in-app + a blog post).
- **L, Multi-channel**: select tactics with targeted reach; a solid GTM plan across several channels.
- **XL, Full GTM**: press, events, demos, broad paid media; reserved for the most significant launches.

## Channels

- **Owned**: channels a company fully controls (email, in-app messages, push, social handles). In almost every plan; only reach people already in your ecosystem.
- **Earned**: channels you don't control but can optimise for (press, reviews, word of mouth, social mentions). The most credible, others talking about you, and the hardest to manufacture.
- **Paid**: channels that require budget to reach beyond your base (paid search, paid social, influencer marketing). Used when owned channels alone can't hit the goal.
- **The channel follows the audience**: match each channel to where your audience already is, not the other way around.

## Enablement

- **Enablement brief**: the document that ensures every internal stakeholder knows what's launching and how it works. At minimum: product description, key features, common objections, what's changing.
- **Enablement assets**: the tangible deliverables to build (one-pagers and demos for all tiers; sales slides and customer stories for sales-led; webinars and product tours for XL).
- **Launch tracker**: the living source of truth: action items + owners, risks to monitor, and a key-decisions log.

## Execution

- **Ownership**: every GTM task needs a *named individual*, not a team. A task owned by everyone is owned by no one.
- **Budget ask**: anything that costs money (media spend, influencer fees, asset production) must be requested *before* the plan is finalised.
- **Asset vs. resource gap**: every asset needs someone to build it and the skill/tool to build it with; a launch video with no producer won't get made.
- **AI launch toolkit**: content & assets (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini), design & visuals (Canva AI, Gamma), automation agents (n8n, Zapier), analytics & signals (GA4, Mixpanel, Amplitude). AI removes the bottleneck of not having a marketing team, it doesn't replace one.
- **What AI can't replace**: genuine market intuition, contrarian strategic bets, and relationships that build trust.

## The three launch phases

- **Phase 1 · Beta / early access**: invite feedback, pressure-test messaging, build advocates. Track: retention curve + feature adoption.
- **Phase 2 · Launch moment**: create the broadest awareness moment; all internal teams live. Track: activation rate among early users.
- **Phase 3 · Post-launch adoption**: convert awareness into long-term behaviour. Track: adoption velocity + error rate.

## Post-launch metrics & decisions

- **Match metrics to the goal**: awareness (reach, signups, landing-page visits), engagement (feature adoption, DAU, time to first key action), conversion (conversion rate, trial-to-paid, retention).
- **Bad signal**: a diagnostic pattern: high reach + zero signups = message-market mismatch; signups + low time-to-first-action = onboarding failure; trials + no upgrades = pricing/value-prop problem.
- **Time to first key action (TFA)**: how quickly a new user reaches their first meaningful moment with the feature.
- **The four post-launch decisions**: **double-down** (sustained signal confirms it's working), **iterate** (mixed signals; change one variable), **pivot** (unexpected results; adjust positioning/audience/product), **deprioritize** (weak signals; log learnings and redirect resources).
- **Post-launch iteration vs. experiment result**: an experiment outcome is a controlled test; post-launch iteration reads *real market behaviour over time*.

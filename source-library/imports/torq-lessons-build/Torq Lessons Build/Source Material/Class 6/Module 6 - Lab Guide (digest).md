## Purpose
Turn the feature scoped in M4 and validated in M5 into a complete GTM plan — this becomes the "Launch" slide of the final deliverables deck.

## Scenario pick (same 3-option pattern as M5: StreamLine / RouteLogic / Own product)

## Structure (pre-work + 5 steps — mirrors the Builder's steps exactly)

**Pre-Work (60 sec):** the feature being launched (from M4 PRD, one feature not a list), what the M5 experiment concluded (shipped/iterating — the evidence behind the launch), and the M2 persona (anchors the audience).

**Step 1 — Goal & audience.** Pick one primary goal (awareness/engagement/conversion), justify it, then define the precise audience it implies.
> **Explicit trap flagged:** defaulting to "awareness" for every launch. Awareness is right for new markets/repositioning; a feature living inside the existing product is probably engagement or conversion, with an audience *internal* to the user base.

**Step 2 — Launch tier.** S/M/L/XL sizing by reach + revenue impact + risk of silence, plus a **launch-vs-release** check: does this actually need go-to-market, or can it just ship?

**Step 3 — Channels & enablement.** 3 channels (each tagged owned/earned/paid + why it reaches the audience), plus enablement brief/assets needed by Sales/CS/Support.
> **Channel check callout:** if the goal is re-engaging lapsed users, in-app messages won't reach them — they're not opening the product. Match channel to where the audience already is.

**Step 4 — Ownership, budget & timeline.** Named owner per activity (individual, not department), budget/asset gaps flagged before lock, and a 3-phase timeline (beta → launch moment → post-launch).

**Step 5 — Success metrics.** 2-3 metrics matched to the goal, a named "bad signal" per metric, and the most likely post-launch decision (double-down/iterate/pivot/deprioritize) + its trigger.

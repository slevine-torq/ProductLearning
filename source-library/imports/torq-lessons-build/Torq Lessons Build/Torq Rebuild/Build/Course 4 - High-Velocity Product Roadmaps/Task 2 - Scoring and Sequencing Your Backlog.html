Scoring and sequencing your backlog

A hands-on lab: get an AI baseline on your real backlog fast, then override it with the evidence only you have.

Course High-Velocity Product Roadmaps Hands-on ~25 min Artifact Now/Next/Later Roadmap

Pull a real backlog from your current engagement — ten to fifteen items is plenty. This lab scores it against effort and value using AI as a fast first pass, then puts your own judgment in charge of the final call.

Before you start

Confirm your engagement's AI policy before pasting real backlog details into an AI tool. If that's off-limits here, use a backlog from a product you know well as a user instead — the discipline being practiced is the same either way.

Three-step scoring lab

Step 1 · Set a human baseline

Flag two or three instinctive quick wins

Before touching AI, scan the backlog and flag two or three items that look like obvious quick wins to you. You'll check the AI's output against this baseline in Step 3.

Step 2 · Get an AI baseline

Deploy the scoring prompt

Fill in your real anchors and backlog, then run it.

Act as a senior product manager scoring a backlog against effort and value.

Strategic anchors:
- Success metric: [the metric this initiative is actually trying to move]
- Guardrail metric: [what must not get worse]
- Constraints: [team size, timeline, or other real limits]

For each item, give:
- Value (1-5): 1 = doesn't touch the success metric, 5 = directly moves it
- Effort (1-5): 1 = days of work, 5 = a major build
- Quadrant: Quick Win / Major Project / Fill-In / Time Sinker
- One-sentence rationale tied to the anchors above, not general best practice

Be ruthless about items that came from an internal ask rather than real evidence.

Backlog:
[paste your real backlog items here]
Step 3 · Audit and override

Your judgment is the actual deliverable

Compare the AI's scores against your Step 1 instincts. Where did it inflate an item that no real evidence backs up? Where did it undervalue something your research already supports? Override the scores, finalize the quadrants, and sort into Now, Next, Later, and Cut.

The AI gives you speed, not the call

It can score fast and defend its reasoning. It can't tell the difference between a request backed by real evidence and one that's just loud. That's still entirely on you.

Key takeaways

  1. AI is fast at scoring, not at knowing what's real. It can't distinguish evidence-backed requests from loud ones — that audit stays yours.
  2. A short Now lane is a sign of discipline, not timidity. If everything's a quick win, nothing actually is.
  3. Every cut needs a written reason. That's what stops a killed item from quietly resurfacing next sprint.
  4. The override, not the score, is the real deliverable. It's the one place your judgment is visible on the page.

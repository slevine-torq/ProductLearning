Reading and building a journey map

A journey map's job is to show exactly where an experience breaks down. Most engagements already have one somewhere — usually stale, sometimes wrong, always worth reading before you draw a new one.

Course Discovering Product Opportunities Hands-on ~25 min Artifact Future-State Journey Map

A journey map is nothing more than what a user does, thinks, and feels at each step of getting something done. Its value is entirely in the low points — the exact moment things go wrong is the exact place your next fix should aim.

Six steps to a working map

  1. Set the goal. Name the specific start and end point of the task you're mapping — not "use the product," but one concrete job.
  2. Choose one persona. Map for a single user type at a time. Mixing personas produces a map that's accurate for nobody.
  3. Define the scope. Are you mapping how it works today, or the future state you're proposing? Don't blend the two on one map.
  4. Outline the steps. List the sequence of actions, and at each one, what the user is doing, thinking, and feeling.
  5. Add the touchpoints. Every place they interact with the product, your team, or a tool outside it — friction often hides in the handoffs between them.
  6. Spot the key moments. Mark where the emotional line dips or spikes. Those points are your map's entire payoff.

What a real gap looks like

Real example

Spotify has publicly discussed mapping its music-sharing flow across several stages — from opening the app to a friend actually hearing what was shared. The emotional line held steady through most of the journey, then dropped sharply at the point where a recipient had to open a separate app just to listen.

The fix followed the gap directly: the friction wasn't in deciding to share, it was in what happened right after — and that's exactly where the product work went.

Notice the map didn't reveal a mystery. It made a known-but-unmeasured pain point impossible to ignore by showing precisely where the line dropped.

Read theirs before you draw your own

If the account already has a journey map, don't start from a blank page — audit the one that exists. Check its date against anything that's shipped since, check whether it was built from real behavior or from guesses in a workshop, and check whether the "key moments" still match what Task 1's evidence actually shows.

Regulated client?

On a healthcare, aerospace & defense, or financial-services engagement, an existing journey map may have been built before a compliance requirement was added — a step that looks like unnecessary friction on the map might be a non-negotiable review gate. Confirm before you propose cutting it.

Key takeaways

  1. A journey map's value is entirely in the low points. The dip in the emotional line is the map's whole payoff.
  2. Map one persona, one scope, at a time. Blending personas or blending current-state with future-state produces a map that's accurate for nobody.
  3. Read an existing map before drawing a new one. Check its age and its evidence before trusting what it says is broken.
  4. A future-state map should show what's eliminated, not just what's added. If the old workaround still exists after launch, the map didn't do its job.

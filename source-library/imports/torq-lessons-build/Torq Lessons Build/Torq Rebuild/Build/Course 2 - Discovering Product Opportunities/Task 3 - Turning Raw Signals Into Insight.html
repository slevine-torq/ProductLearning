Turning raw signals into insight

A hands-on lab: run a pile of real, messy notes through an AI synthesis pass, then audit the result against your own read of the same material.

Course Discovering Product Opportunities Hands-on ~15 min Artifact Signal Synthesis Brief

Gather whatever raw research exists on your engagement — interview notes, support tickets, call transcripts, a client's own research doc. This lab runs it through an AI synthesis pass, then checks whether the summary actually held onto what mattered or quietly smoothed it away.

Before you start

Confirm your engagement's AI policy and approved tools before putting any client specifics into an AI tool. If client detail is off-limits here, run this on a product you know well as a user instead — the lesson is about the audit discipline, not the specific subject matter.

The three-step synthesis check

Step 1 · Set your baseline

Read it yourself, first

Spend five minutes reading the raw material before touching AI. Don't organize it yet — just note the two or three moments that stand out as real, specific pain. This is what you'll check the AI's output against.

Step 2 · Run the synthesis

Deploy the prompt

Open a fresh chat and run this, with your own raw notes pasted in where indicated.

Act as a product analyst reviewing raw research on my behalf.

Task: read the material below and produce a structured summary grouped by
theme. For each theme, note how many separate sources raised it and how
severe the friction sounds.

Constraints:
- Do not propose solutions, features, or a roadmap. Themes only.
- Do not invent or infer anything not directly supported by the text.
- If two sources conflict, note the conflict rather than resolving it.
- Flag anything you're uncertain how to categorize instead of guessing.

Raw material:
[paste your notes here]

The prompt deliberately excludes solutions. Evidence isn't the same thing as a decision — asking for themes only keeps the actual prioritization call with you, where it belongs.

Step 3 · Audit it

Check the output against your baseline

Did it catch what you caught?

Compare the themes against your own two or three standouts from Step 1. If something you flagged got folded into a generic bullet, that's a real loss — note it.

Did it stay in its lane?

Check for anywhere it slipped in a recommendation, a feature idea, or an inferred detail the source material didn't actually support. That's the thing your audit exists to catch.

Trust, but verify

A synthesis tool can organize what's in front of it. It can't tell you which theme is worth a sprint versus which one is a minor complaint from one loud account — that call runs through the discovery filter from Task 1, and it stays yours.

Key takeaways

  1. Read it yourself before you automate it. Without a baseline, you can't tell whether the AI's summary is accurate or just fluent.
  2. Ask for themes, not solutions. Keeping the roadmap out of the prompt keeps the actual decision with you.
  3. The audit is the deliverable, not the summary. A synthesis tool organizes evidence — it doesn't decide what matters.
  4. What got smoothed away is often the most important finding. A detail an AI compressed into a generic bullet was often the sharpest part of the signal.

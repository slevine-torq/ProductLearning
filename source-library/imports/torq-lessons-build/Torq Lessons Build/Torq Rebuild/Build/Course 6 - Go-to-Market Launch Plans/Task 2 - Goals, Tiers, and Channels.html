Goals, tiers, and channels

Three decisions, made in order: what this launch needs to accomplish, how big a moment it deserves, and where the right people will actually see it.

Course Go-to-Market Launch Plans Read time ~9 min Artifact Launch Framing Worksheet

Skip straight to channels and you'll pick the wrong ones. Goal, then tier, then channel — each decision constrains the next.

Start with the goal

Every launch goal maps to a stage of the funnel you already know from Course 3 — and the action a PM takes looks different at each one.

GoalWho you're reachingWhat you're actually doing
AwarenessPeople who don't know this exists yetGetting the thing in front of eyes — visibility is the entire job
EngagementPeople who've seen it but haven't tried itGiving them a reason and a clear next step to actually use it
ConversionPeople actively evaluating itRemoving the last friction between interest and a real commitment

Naming the wrong goal is the single most common launch-planning mistake — a launch built for awareness measured against a conversion target will always look like it failed.

Size the moment: launch tiers

Borrowing the same t-shirt-sizing instinct you'd use to size any piece of work, not every launch deserves the same level of production.

SA quiet rollout, minimal fanfare
MA real announcement, modest campaign
LCross-functional push, dedicated assets
XLCompany-wide moment, full campaign

Launch ≠ release. A release can ship silently to a small segment; a launch is a deliberate choice to make noise about it. Confusing the two is how a minor update gets an XL-sized push it doesn't need, or a genuinely important change gets none at all.

Where the message travels: owned, earned, paid

A standard media framework, useful for any launch tier: three channel types, each with a different cost and a different kind of credibility.

Owned

Channels you fully control — your product's in-app messaging, your email list, your blog. No cost per message, but only reaches people already paying attention.

Earned

Attention you didn't pay for — press coverage, word of mouth, organic social sharing. Highest credibility, least controllable.

Paid

Advertising and sponsored placement. Fastest to scale, most predictable, but stops the moment the budget does.

Most real launches blend all three — an owned announcement, earned press pickup, and a paid push to extend reach past your existing audience.

Key takeaways

  1. Goal, then tier, then channel — never skip ahead. Each decision only makes sense in light of the one before it.
  2. Launch ≠ release. One is a deployment decision, the other is a communication decision — mixing them up wastes effort in both directions.
  3. Owned, earned, and paid do different jobs. Most real launches need all three, not just whichever one is easiest to execute.

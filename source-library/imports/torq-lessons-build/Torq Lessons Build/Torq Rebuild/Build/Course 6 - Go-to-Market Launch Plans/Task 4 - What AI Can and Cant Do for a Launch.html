What AI can and can't do for a launch

AI can draft your launch assets in minutes. It has no opinion on whether this is the right week to launch, or whether the market will actually care. That judgment is still yours.

Course Go-to-Market Launch Plans Read time ~7 min Artifact AI Task Split

The pattern from Course 1 holds all the way through the program: AI provides synthesis, the PM provides discernment. A launch is where that split matters most, because the cost of getting it wrong is public.

Where AI genuinely helps

  • Content and assets — first drafts of announcement copy, FAQ entries, sales one-pagers, and email sequences, ready for a human edit pass.
  • Design and visuals — quick mockups and variations for internal review, not final production assets.
  • Automation — scheduling posts, populating enablement docs, routing tickets to the right queue during a launch window.
  • Analytics and signals — surfacing early post-launch numbers fast, the way you'd use it in Course 3's diagnostic work.

What no tool replaces

  • Market intuition — reading whether this is genuinely the right moment, audience, and framing, informed by context no prompt fully captures.
  • Contrarian bets — the judgment call to launch against convention when the data is ambiguous and the upside is real.
  • Trust relationships — the internal credibility and external relationships that make a launch land with the people who matter, built over time, not generated on demand.
The same discipline as Course 5

Faster drafts and faster synthesis are not the same thing as better judgment — the exact caution from Course 5's Task 1 on experimentation culture. A launch plan assembled entirely from AI-generated pieces, with no human editorial judgment about tone, timing, or fit, reads like exactly what it is.

Key takeaways

  1. AI accelerates drafts, not decisions. The same split you learned in Course 1 applies here at full scale.
  2. Market intuition and trust relationships aren't promptable. They come from context and history a tool doesn't have.
  3. A visibly all-AI launch plan reads as exactly that. Human editorial judgment on tone and timing is what makes it land.

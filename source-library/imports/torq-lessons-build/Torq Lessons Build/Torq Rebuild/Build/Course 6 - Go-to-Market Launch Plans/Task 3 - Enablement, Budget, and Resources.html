Enablement, budget, and resources

A launch plan that never leaves the product team fails the moment a customer calls Support and gets a shrug. Enablement is what prevents that — and it has to happen before day one, not after.

Course Go-to-Market Launch Plans Read time ~8 min Artifact Enablement Brief

Every team that touches a customer after launch needs to know what changed before that customer does.

Who needs to be ready, and with what

The internal brief is the single artifact that gets everyone else current — written once, shared before the launch moment, not reconstructed by each team from whatever they overheard.

  • Sales What's new, who it's for, and how to position it against what a prospect already knows — not a feature list, a story they can actually tell.
  • Customer Success What existing customers should expect, and what to proactively flag before it becomes a surprised email.
  • Support What's likely to generate a ticket, and the answer ready before the first one arrives — not written reactively after it does.

The specific assets scale with the launch tier from Task 2 — an S-tier release might need a two-line Slack update; an XL moment needs a real briefing deck and a Q&A doc. The discipline of writing it down doesn't scale down, even when the tier does.

Resources and budget

Every launch draws on two finite things: people's time and actual spend. Naming both explicitly — who owns the launch, what's been approved to spend, which assets already exist versus need to be built — is what separates a plan from a hope.

One level up

Budget sign-off and named ownership for a launch are usually decided above the day-to-day delivery team — often settled when the engagement itself was scoped into the SOW, long before this task's checklist gets filled in. You're not expected to make that call. You are expected to know it was made, by whom, and what it actually authorized — because a launch plan built against an assumption of budget that was never approved is a plan that stalls in week one.

Key takeaways

  1. Enablement has to happen before the launch moment, not after. The first support ticket is too late to start writing the answer.
  2. Assets scale with tier — the discipline doesn't. Even an S-tier release deserves a written, shared brief.
  3. Budget and ownership are often decided above the delivery team. Knowing that decision was made, and by whom, is part of the job even when making it isn't.

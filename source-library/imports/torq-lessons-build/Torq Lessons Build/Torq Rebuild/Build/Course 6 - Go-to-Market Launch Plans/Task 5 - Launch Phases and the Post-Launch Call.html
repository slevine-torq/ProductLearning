Launch phases and the post-launch call

The launch moment is a milestone, not the finish line. What happens in the weeks after is where you find out whether the plan actually worked.

Course Go-to-Market Launch Plans Read time ~9 min Artifact Post-Launch Decision Log

A launch has a before, a during, and an after. Most of the real learning happens in the after — if you're watching for it.

Three phases

  1. Beta / early access A small group gets it first, under real conditions. This is where you catch what your Course 5 experiments couldn't — problems that only surface at genuine, uncontrolled usage.
  2. Launch moment The tier-appropriate push from Task 2, backed by the enablement work from Task 3. Everything before this phase was preparation; this is where it's tested against a real audience.
  3. Post-launch adoption The weeks after, where the actual signal comes in — separate from the noise of launch-day excitement.

Post-launch metrics, matched to your goal

The goal you named in Task 2 tells you which stage of Course 3's AARRR funnel to actually watch after launch — not everything at once.

GoalWatch
AwarenessAcquisition — are new people actually finding it
EngagementActivation — are they reaching the moment of real value
ConversionRevenue / Referral — are they committing, and telling others

The call: four post-launch decisions

Double-down

Working as hoped — commit more resourcing behind it.

Iterate

Real signal, not enough of it yet — refine and keep going.

Pivot

The core idea holds, the execution doesn't — change direction, keep the validated insight.

Deprioritize

No real signal after a genuine test — redirect effort elsewhere.

"Pivot" isn't a vague word here

Eric Ries coined "pivot" in The Lean Startup to mean something specific: changing direction while keeping what you validated. It's not "we're confused, let's try something else" — it's "this part of the hypothesis held, this part didn't, so we're changing course based on that evidence." Using it loosely is how a real decision framework turns into a shrug.

Key takeaways

  1. The launch moment isn't the finish line. Most of the real signal arrives in the weeks after.
  2. Your goal decides which metric to watch post-launch. Awareness, engagement, and conversion each point to a different part of the funnel.
  3. "Pivot" means something specific. Keep what the evidence validated, change what it didn't — not a synonym for giving up.

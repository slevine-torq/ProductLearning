Scenario: the Loopline onboarding step

You've been working the Loopline account through this program — the roadmap in Course 4 Task 1, the MoSCoW scope in Task 3, the PRD in Task 4. One line from that PRD is still just an assumption: the team believes a shorter setup flow will get more new teams to invite a second teammate in their first week, which is the outcome-level signal from Course 3 that actually predicts long-term retention for a helpdesk tool like Loopline.

Nobody's tested it. That's this lab. If you're not working a Loopline scenario, swap in your own — a real feature you've scoped, with a real metric attached — and fill the brief in around that instead.

Build the brief, step by step

  1. Ground it in a real metric

    Go back to the metric you chose in Course 3. What's the outcome, and what proxy or leading indicator will you actually watch during the test?

  2. Pull the change from your scoped work

    Look at what you scoped in Course 4 — the MVP cut from the MoSCoW exercise, or a specific PRD requirement. Pick one discrete, testable change from it.

  3. Write the hypothesis

    Use the formula from Task 3: the change, the persona, the expected outcome, the size and timeframe, the guardrail that must hold.

  4. Pick the method and justify it

    Use the matching table from Task 2. Given your traffic and the risk of this change, which of the four methods fits — and why not the others?

  5. Set control, variant, and guardrails

    Control is the product as it exists in the Course 4 PRD today. The variant is your one change. Name at least one metric you're not willing to let slip.

Key takeaways

  1. A brief is only as good as its inputs. Pulling from real Course 3/4 work — not a made-up scenario — is what makes this exercise transfer to actual work.
  2. One change, one hypothesis, one primary metric. If your brief needs "and" to describe the change, it's two tests wearing one brief.
  3. The method choice needs a stated reason. "Because it's what we always do" isn't a justification — traffic and risk are.

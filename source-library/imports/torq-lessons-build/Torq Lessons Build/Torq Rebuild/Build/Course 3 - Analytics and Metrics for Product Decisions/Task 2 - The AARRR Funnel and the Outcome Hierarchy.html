The AARRR funnel & the outcome hierarchy

A five-stage map of the user lifecycle, and a reminder that on any account with more than one type of user, there's more than one funnel running at once.

Course Analytics & Metrics for Product Decisions Read time ~8 min Artifact Funnel Ownership Map

Before you can decide which metric to move, you need to know where in the user's lifecycle you're actually trying to move it. This framework — widely known as "Pirate Metrics" since Dave McClure named it in the late 2000s — pins that down in five stages.

The five stages

StageWhat it answersTypical metric
AcquisitionAre people showing up?Signup rate
ActivationDid they experience the value fast?Time to first key action
RetentionDid it become a habit?Returning users over time
RevenueDid that value convert to business sustainability?Lifetime value
ReferralDid it get good enough to spread on its own?Net promoter score

Two stages you actually control

Acquisition is mostly a marketing and brand question. Revenue and referral are downstream consequences of everything upstream working. Activation and retention are different — they're the two stages a product build can move most directly, which is why most of a product team's diagnostic energy belongs there.

When a metric outside those two stages is underperforming, that's usually a sign to loop in whoever owns acquisition or growth — not to assume it's a product problem to fix alone.

One funnel per user type, not one funnel total

On any product with more than one kind of user — a marketplace, a platform, anything with a supply side and a demand side — "improve the funnel" is meaningless until you name which user's funnel you mean. Each type runs its own version of Acquisition through Revenue, and they're often coupled: one side's activation depends on the other side already being active.

Illustrative pattern

Picture a two-sided content platform: viewers, creators, and advertisers. A viewer's funnel is about finding something worth watching fast. A creator's funnel is about getting a first upload seen. An advertiser's funnel is about a first campaign actually converting. Advertiser spend only grows if creators keep producing, and creators only keep producing if viewers keep showing up — so a metric that looks flat on the advertiser side might trace back to a leak on the viewer side entirely.

Before proposing a fix, know which funnel is actually broken, and which funnel just inherited the symptom from somewhere upstream.

Key takeaways

  1. AARRR names five lifecycle stages, one at a time. Knowing which one you're actually diagnosing keeps you from treating a symptom as the disease.
  2. Activation and retention are where product work has real leverage. Acquisition and revenue mostly depend on what happens upstream and downstream of the build.
  3. More than one user type means more than one funnel. Naming which one is broken is step one, not an afterthought.
  4. Funnels on a multi-sided product are coupled. A weak metric on one side is often a downstream symptom of a leak on another.

Slicing and sizing the problem

A single number tells you something is wrong. It never tells you where, or how bad. Two techniques answer those, and they answer different questions.

Course Analytics & Metrics for Product Decisions Read time ~9 min Artifact Slice & Size Worksheet

An aggregate metric is a average hiding a story. Segmentation finds where the pain actually lives. Cohort analysis tells you whether it's getting worse, staying flat, or improving. You need both — one without the other leaves you guessing.

Segmentation: finding where

A metric on its own tells you what happened. Breaking it down by a real trait tells you where — and the average almost always hides more than it reveals. Four lenses cover most of what matters:

  • Behavior — does the friction hit one specific action in a sequence, rather than the whole flow?
  • Tenure — is the drop-off happening to brand-new users, or to people who've been around for months?
  • Environment — is it isolated to one platform, device, or region, rather than universal?
  • Account tier — does it hit everyone, or just users on a particular plan or access level?

A product that looks healthy in aggregate can still be quietly failing one segment badly enough to matter. The average is the thing you look past, not the thing you report.

Reading a dashboard past the top row

The top-line numbers — total customers, average value, overall growth — are result signals. They tell you the scoreboard, not what to do next. The real decisions live in the segments underneath.

Cohort analysis: sizing how urgent

A cohort is a group of users who all started at the same time, tracked separately so you can compare their trajectory to everyone else's. Two patterns tell you what kind of problem you have:

  • Decline — a leak Newer cohorts drop off faster than older ones. The product is getting worse for new users specifically, and it needs a fix, not a new idea.
  • Flatline — a ceiling Every cohort, old and new, stalls at the same point. The product is stable but stuck, and a fix won't break through it — you need a genuine improvement.

Reading a cohort heatmap horizontally shows one group's trajectory over time; reading it vertically compares different groups at the same age. The earliest drop — usually the first week — is typically the most controllable lever, and a cohort trending toward zero is a sign that acquisition spend is being wasted on a leaking bucket.

Using AI as a first pass, not the last word

Export the relevant slice of data — by segment, by cohort, by feature usage — and ask a model to surface what's underperforming or what shifted between two periods. It's fast at finding candidate patterns. It isn't the one deciding which pattern is worth acting on; that judgment call is still yours.

Key takeaways

  1. Segmentation finds where; cohort analysis sizes how urgent. You need both — one alone leaves a real gap in the picture.
  2. The average is what you look past. A healthy top-line number regularly hides a segment that's quietly failing.
  3. A decline needs a fix; a flatline needs an improvement. Treating one like the other wastes the move.
  4. AI can find the candidate pattern fast. Deciding which pattern is worth acting on is still a human call.

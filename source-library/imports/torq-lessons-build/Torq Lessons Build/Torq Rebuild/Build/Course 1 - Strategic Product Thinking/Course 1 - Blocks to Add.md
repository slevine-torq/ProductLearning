## Task 1 — The Modern PM Mindset

### Block: Mindset Key Terms — Type: Card flipping

1. Term: Output | Definition: What you shipped — a feature live, a release out the door. Says nothing on its own about whether it worked.
2. Term: Outcome | Definition: The measurable change in user behavior or business result a release was supposed to produce. What actually justifies the release.
3. Term: North Star | Definition: A single shared direction that tells the team what to optimize for. Without one, everything looks equally urgent and nothing is really prioritized.
4. Term: Desirability | Definition: Whether a real user has the problem you'd be solving, and whether it hurts enough to change their behavior for it.
5. Term: Viability | Definition: Whether solving the problem is worth the resources — revenue protected, cost avoided, or a strategic position defended.
6. Term: Feasibility | Definition: Whether the fix can actually be built, shipped, and kept running given the time, skills, and constraints you have.

### Block: Mindset Check — Type: Quiz

1. What is the usual root cause when a product team feels scattered and directionless?
   A. Not enough engineering capacity
   B. No agreed single direction — without one, everything looks equally urgent ← correct
   C. Stakeholders submitting too many requests
   D. The backlog tool being poorly configured

2. A feature ships on time, exactly as specced, and the target metric doesn't move. What happened?
   A. A success — the team delivered what was asked
   B. A partial success — the date was hit
   C. The output landed but the outcome didn't; a date isn't evidence of impact ← correct
   D. A measurement error

3. A fix is desirable and feasible, but nobody can point to what it protects or grows. What's missing?
   A. Desirability
   B. Feasibility
   C. Viability ← correct
   D. Nothing — two out of three is enough

4. Where does the highest-impact work sit?
   A. Wherever the loudest stakeholder is pointing
   B. Where desirability, viability, and feasibility all clear at once ← correct
   C. Wherever effort is lowest
   D. Wherever a competitor has already shipped

5. An initiative is viable and feasible, but no user has actually asked for it or would notice it. What's missing?
   A. Desirability ← correct
   B. Feasibility
   C. Viability
   D. Nothing — it's ready to build

## Task 4 — The Product Development Life Cycle

### Block: Place the Stage — Type: Card classification

**Buckets:** Early, Execution, Final

1. "Discovery" → Early
2. "Research" → Early
3. "Design" → Execution
4. "Development" → Execution
5. "Testing" → Execution
6. "Launch" → Final
7. "Iteration" → Final

### Block: Lifecycle Key Terms — Type: Card flipping

1. Term: PDLC | Definition: The Product Development Life Cycle — the repeatable map from a hunch to a shipped product: Discovery → Research → Design → Development → Testing → Launch → Iteration.
2. Term: Discovery | Definition: The first phase — gathering signals from market, users, and business strategy to find an opportunity worth expensive resources.
3. Term: Research | Definition: Validating the opportunity through data and interviews, and defining what a winning outcome looks like before work begins.
4. Term: Iteration | Definition: Using real post-launch behaviour to decide whether to double down, pivot, or move on. Shipping starts a data loop rather than ending the work.
5. Term: AI-powered life cycle | Definition: Using AI to move through the PDLC faster — synthesising inputs, drafting artifacts, generating launch material — while the high-value decisions stay human.

## Task 5 — Your Problem Hook & Value Proposition

### Block: Framing the Why — Type: Card flipping

1. Term: Problem hook | Definition: A single urgent sentence fusing the business risk with the user's pain, explaining why this must be solved now.
2. Term: Value proposition | Definition: Who a fix is for, the specific gain it delivers over their current state, and why it's worth acting on now rather than later.
3. Term: Strategic crisis | Definition: The high-level business risk — what the company stands to lose if the status quo continues for another 12 months.
4. Term: Moment of misery | Definition: The specific point where a product fails a user, forcing a manual workaround. The human anchor of a problem hook.
5. Term: Cold-read test | Definition: Stepping away, then reading your own hook silently as a skeptical stakeholder would. If you don't feel the urgency, that's your data.

### Block: Sharpen the Hook — Type: Quiz

1. Which is a usable strategic crisis?
   A. "Engagement could be higher."
   B. "Users have asked for improvements."
   C. "If usage doesn't recover by Q3, we enter the renewal conversation without a case." ← correct
   D. "The product feels dated."

2. Which is a real moment of misery?
   A. "The tool is slow."
   B. "The coordinator re-enters the same data into three systems every morning before the shift starts." ← correct
   C. "Users are frustrated."
   D. "The interface needs work."

3. Your problem hook takes three sentences to explain. What does that tell you?
   A. It's thorough
   B. You haven't found the hook yet — it should fuse risk and pain in one sentence ← correct
   C. It needs a slide deck
   D. The audience isn't technical enough

4. What's the point of the cold-read test?
   A. To check spelling and grammar
   B. To time how long it takes to read
   C. To hear it the way a skeptical stakeholder will — with no context you'd normally add out loud ← correct
   D. To get a colleague's approval before presenting

5. Your value proposition could describe almost any feature on the roadmap. What's the problem?
   A. Nothing — broad applicability is a strength
   B. It isn't specific enough yet; generic value props get generic funding decisions ← correct
   C. It should be longer
   D. It needs more technical detail

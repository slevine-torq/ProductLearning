## What Torq is

**Torq** ([gotorq.com](https://www.gotorq.com)) — a boutique management consulting firm, ~36 employees, founded 2020, headquartered in Plano, TX. Now a subsidiary of **Sendero Consulting**. Tagline: "Paving a New Way Forward."

**Not** the security-automation company of the same name (torq.io) — different company, easy to confuse, confirmed via direct research 2026-08-21.

### Practices
- Product Strategy & Digital Products
- Software Development & Digital Engineering
- Data & Analytics
- Enterprise Transformation

### Target clients
Fortune 500 companies across six primary industries: **automotive, airlines, healthcare, consumer services/food & beverage, aerospace & defense, financial services.** Several of these (healthcare, aerospace & defense, financial services) are regulated — compliance/approval-cycle constraints show up on client engagements in a way that doesn't apply to a typical in-house tech-company PM.

### Consultant roles & profile
Roles include "Consultant – Product Management" and "Product Owner," typically **3–5 years of experience**, staffed onto client engagements. Positioning language: "consulting isn't a service; it's a true partnership."

Sources: [gotorq.com](https://www.gotorq.com), [Built In — Torq Careers, Perks + Culture](https://builtin.com/company/torq-gotorqcom), [Greenhouse — Jobs at Torq](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/gotorq)

## What this means for any Torq L&D build (not PMC-specific)

The learner is **not** an embedded FTE PM who owns one product indefinitely. They are a consultant who:

- Gets **staffed onto someone else's team** for a fixed engagement and has to be credible fast — no months of ramp time
- Is often **advising or temporarily filling a gap**, not personally holding roadmap/budget authority
- Works across **regulated and non-regulated industries** in the same year — a framework that's safe at a consumer/F&B client may not translate cleanly to a financial-services or aerospace/defense client
- Needs to **hand off cleanly** — the value of the engagement often lives on after Torq leaves, so specs/artifacts must work without the consultant in the room
- Is judged on **client-visible deliverables and readouts**, not internal product metrics over a multi-year tenure

**Design implication for every course:** every lesson should answer "what do I do in the client's room," not "what does a PM own," and should end in an artifact the consultant can pull up and use on an engagement (template, canvas, checklist) — not just a concept they now understand. Any content borrowed from Product School (or any FTE-oriented source) needs an explicit audience-swap pass before it's reused: wherever the source says "you decide," ask whether a Torq consultant actually has that authority on a client engagement, or whether the real skill is *influencing* someone else's decision.

## Regulated-industry callout pattern

Any module touching data privacy, compliance, or long approval cycles should carry a callout box, e.g.:

> **Regulated client?** If you're engaged with a healthcare, aerospace & defense, or financial-services client, this step likely needs [legal/compliance review, longer approval cycle, data-handling constraints] before you can move forward the way this lesson describes.

Reuse this pattern verbatim across courses rather than inventing new phrasing per lesson.

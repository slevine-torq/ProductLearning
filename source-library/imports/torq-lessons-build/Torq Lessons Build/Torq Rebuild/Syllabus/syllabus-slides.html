Course 15 tasks · 5 artifacts

Strategic Product Thinking

  • A shared North Star is the antidote to "everything is urgent."When nothing is prioritized, the problem is usually clarity, not capacity.
  • Plausible output is the dangerous kind.An obviously wrong AI suggestion gets caught — a reasonable-looking feature nobody asked for gets shipped.
  • The lifecycle exists to catch a wrong bet early.Each stage is a checkpoint where a bad idea is still cheap to kill.
Ask an AI for a roadmap and it'll hand you a clean-looking list in seconds. Whether any of it deserves engineering time is a separate question, and it's the one you're actually paid to answer.

Course 25 tasks · 5 artifacts

Discovering Product Opportunities

  • AI compressed the synthesis, not the judgment.Deciding what a finding means and whether it's worth acting on is still entirely your call.
  • Behavior beats demographics as a persona anchor.What someone's trying to do predicts product decisions — age and title don't.
  • Inaction is usually the real competitor.Status-quo bias is documented behavior, not a special quirk of your account.
The account most likely to beat your initiative isn't a rival vendor. It's the client deciding their current spreadsheet is safer than the risk of switching.

Course 35 tasks · 5 artifacts

Analytics & Metrics for Product Decisions

  • A rising number isn't automatically good news.Test whether it could climb while the user is objectively worse off.
  • Segmentation finds where; cohort analysis sizes how urgent.You need both — one alone leaves a real gap in the picture.
  • A hypothesis is a measurable contract, not a wish.Evidence, persona, outcome, metric, and guardrail — all five, or it isn't done yet.
A five-stage map of the user lifecycle, and a reminder that on any account with more than one type of user, there's more than one funnel running at once.

Course 46 tasks · 6 artifacts

High-Velocity Product Roadmaps

  • AI is fast at scoring, not at knowing what's real.It can't distinguish evidence-backed requests from loud ones — that audit stays yours.
  • A lean PRD is stripped down, not shallow.It keeps the same discipline as a traditional PRD, tuned for a faster iteration loop.
  • A PRD describes intent; acceptance criteria describe a test.Handing off only the first one guarantees a round of clarifying questions.
Not a bureaucratic sign-off document — a blueprint precise enough that an AI prototyping tool, or a human engineer, can build from it without guessing.

Course 57 tasks · 7 artifacts

Product Experimentation

  • Every opinion is a hypothesis until it's tested.Including yours, and including the most senior person's in the room.
  • Isolation is what makes a result trustworthy.One variable, identical measurement on both arms.
  • A dashboard result is a claim, not a decision.Five specific questions separate a trustworthy read from a convenient one.
Everything from Tasks 1–3 comes together here. You'll write a real, complete experiment brief — pulling inputs straight from the metrics and roadmap work you've already built in this program.

Course 6 · Capstone7 tasks · 7 artifacts

Go-to-Market Launch Plans

  • A launch plan is seven concrete decisions.Each one made deliberately, not by default — not a marketing afterthought.
  • Enablement has to happen before the launch moment.The first support ticket is too late to start writing the answer.
  • The launch moment isn't the finish line.Most of the real signal arrives in the weeks after.
AI can draft your launch assets in minutes. It has no opinion on whether this is the right week to launch, or whether the market will actually care. That judgment is still yours.

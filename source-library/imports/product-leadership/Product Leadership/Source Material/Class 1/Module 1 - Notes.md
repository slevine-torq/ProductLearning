# Module 1 · Craft an Advanced Product Strategy: Notes

> Product Leadership Certification · shareable class notes. One spine all the way through: **Playing to Win**.

## The leap: IC → product leader

The work that got you here, shipping features well, is not the work that makes you a leader. Four shifts define the transition:

| | From | To |
|---|---|---|
| **Bets** | shipping features | making bets on where to play and how to win |
| **Direction** | executing someone else's direction | setting the direction others execute against |
| **Insights** | being handed analysis | deciding what questions are worth asking |
| **Scope** | owning one product area | strategic decisions across multiple teams |

**Strategy is a set of choices**, not a roadmap or a vision statement: where to play, how to win, and what to say no to.

## The strategy hierarchy

**Vision → Strategy → Roadmap → Execution.** Each layer constrains the one below. Vision is stable and aspirational; execution changes weekly. Blur these and you pay in thrash.

> Vision is the **input**. This module is about the **strategy** layer.

## Three tests for a defensible vision

- **Why now?** What shifted in the last 2 to 3 years? *If the vision would have been equally valid five years ago, it's a description, not a vision.* (Zoom in 2013 vs. 2020.)
- **Why us?** What do we understand that others haven't seen? A durable right to win. (Figma: design as a team sport.)
- **Why us, now?** What must be true inside the org? Healthy tension between ambition and readiness; a complete disconnect is fatal. (A 12-person team owning one city, not "the global standard.")

## Playing to Win: the one spine

Five choices that must cohere top to bottom:

| Question | Decision it forces |
|---|---|
| **Winning aspiration** | Winning in the customer's terms, not internal metrics |
| **Where to play** | Segment, geography, channel, use case, the no's matter |
| **How to win** | A differentiator competitors can't easily replicate |
| **Capabilities required** | What you must be world-class at (build / buy / partner) |
| **Management systems** | Metrics and rituals that reinforce your choices |

> **Keep in mind:** capabilities that sound impressive but don't connect to *how you win* are just noise.

**The process you run** (vision → insights → challenges → approaches → accountability → share early) is *how you produce* the cascade. The cascade is the *output*.

**Rumelt's kernel** lives inside it: **diagnosis** sharpens *Where to Play*, **guiding policy** is *How to Win*, **coherent actions** are the capabilities + systems.

### Strategy autopsy: Figma vs. Adobe XD

- **Figma (win):** diagnosis = siloed, file-based design; guiding policy = make design a team sport, browser-first; coherent actions = in-browser, prototyping + handoff in one file, priced per editor. Every choice reinforced the others, the *no desktop app* constraint became the lever.
- **Adobe XD (fail):** defended the Creative Cloud ecosystem; added features to close gaps but none reinforced a single bet. Assets without a guiding policy is motion, not progress. Discontinued 2023.

### Nested lenses

- **Jobs-to-be-Done** sharpens *Winning Aspiration*, what progress (functional, social, emotional) is the customer hiring us for? (Spotify: the right song for every moment.)
- **Platform vs. Product** sharpens *How to Win*, do we win alone, or make others win with us? (Salesforce → platform ecosystem.)

## AI as your strategic co-pilot

- **Competitive monitoring**: continuous signal instead of a manual scan.
- **Synthesizing**: surface what matters across markets, competitors, customers.
- **Stress-testing**: devil's advocate; the judgment stays yours.

## From strategy to execution

**OKRs are the bridge:** Strategy → **OKRs** → Roadmap → Execution.

- **Bad KR:** "Ship readiness model by Q2" (a delivery milestone).
- **Good KR:** "Race-week retention 34% → 62%" (a measurable result).

*HealthTrack example*, Objective: become the app athletes trust for race-week decisions. KR1 retention 34→62%, KR2 readiness-model opens 1.2→4.5, KR3 NPS 31→58.

## Deciding under ambiguity

1. **Name what you know**: facts vs. interpretation.
2. **Name what you don't**: knowable-with-research vs. live-with.
3. **Check reversibility**: two-way door vs. one-way door.
4. **Provisional bet + trigger**: your best call + what would change your mind.

## Strategy self-diagnostic (6 questions)

Clear? · Names the real challenge? · Makes a hard bet? · Cascadable? · Coherent? · Committed (resources moving)?

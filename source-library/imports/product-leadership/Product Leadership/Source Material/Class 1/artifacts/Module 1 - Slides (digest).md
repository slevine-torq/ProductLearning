## Embedded exercise data / worked examples (verbatim-ish, compressed)

**HealthTrack — Playing to Win cascade example** (used throughout as the running worked example):
- Winning Aspiration: "The app recreational athletes open on race day because it tells them the truth."
- Where to Play: "Recreational runners and cyclists, 28–45, US & UK only. Not elite athletes. Not gym-based fitness."
- How to Win: "Heart-rate, pace, and sleep in one training signal. Six years of data competitors cannot buy."
- Capabilities Required: "Predictive readiness modeling. Garmin and Whoop partnerships. Race-specific content team."
- Management Systems: "Weekly retention by cohort. Monthly readiness-model accuracy. Quarterly win/loss review."

**HealthTrack — OKR cascade example:**
Objective: "Become the app serious recreational athletes trust to make their race-week decisions."
KR1: 7-day race-week retention 34% → 62%. KR2: readiness-model opens/user 1.2 → 4.5. KR3: race-week NPS 31 → 58.

**Strategy Autopsy — Figma (coherent) vs. Adobe XD (incoherent), via Rumelt's kernel:**
- *Figma* — Diagnosis: design was file-based/siloed, handoffs broken, work lost in Slack/email. Guiding Policy: "Make design a team sport," browser-first, multiplayer by default. Coherent Actions: browser launch (no install friction), prototyping+dev-handoff in one file, per-editor pricing. Verdict: no-desktop-app constraint became the strategy's lever.
- *Adobe XD* — Diagnosis: Adobe had distribution advantage, never asked what would convert it into a product worth staying for. Guiding Policy: defend Creative Cloud ecosystem via integration, not product merit. Coherent Actions: bolted on cloud storage/co-editing/prototyping to close gaps, none reinforcing one bet. Verdict: discontinued 2023 — "resources without a guiding policy is motion, not progress."

**Final project scenarios** (consistent with Final Project Brief digest — see that file for full text): Fable (B2C, Growth Wall) and Meridian (B2B, Field Gap), each restated here with a one-line "your competitor" framing (Fable: "the user deciding they don't need Fable at all"; Meridian: "a foreman's phone camera and a group text").

## Reusable AI prompts (exact text)

**AI pressure-test prompt** (used in Sprint 2 lab, section 28):
```
You are a skeptical Chief Strategy Officer reviewing a product strategy draft. Your job is to find where it is soft, vague, or likely to fail, not to validate it.

Here is the strategy:
[PASTE YOUR CASCADE, HARD NO, AND OKRs HERE]

Please:
1. Identify the single biggest assumption I am making that could be wrong.
2. Ask me the one question a board member would ask that I cannot yet answer.
3. Tell me if any of my KRs are actually outputs disguised as outcomes.
4. Point out one thing I said I would not do and make the case for reconsidering.
5. In one sentence: is this a strategy or a wish list? Why?

If your response feels too gentle, add: "Now be harsher: what would a competitor's strategy team say?"
```

## Key interaction logic
- **Scroll-snap presentation mode**: `html { scroll-snap-type: y mandatory }`, each `<section>` full-viewport, `IntersectionObserver` (threshold 0.35) toggles `.visible` and drives progress bar.
- **Nav dots** (right-rail) — one per non-skipped section, active dot highlighted, click-to-scroll, hover tooltip shows section title.
- **Progress bar** — width = (position among *non-skipped* sections) / (total non-skipped sections) × 100%.
- **Section skip system**: `localStorage` key `skip-sections-<sanitized-title>` stores an array of skipped section indices; skip toggle via `K` key or the "skip badge" (bottom-left, appears only when current section is skipped) or the section-sorter UI. Skipped sections get `display:none` and are excluded from nav dots and progress calc.
- **Section sorter** (`M` key) — full-screen overlay grid of all sections with per-section skip toggle and click-to-jump; mirrors current skip state live.
- **Keyboard nav**: ↓/→/Space = next non-skipped section, ↑/← = previous, `K` = toggle skip current, `M`/`Esc` = open/close sorter.
- **Deck-sync (`BroadcastChannel`)**: an instructor deck and its "(Shareable)" twin, opened in the same browser, sync scroll position via a channel keyed on `deck-sync::<dir>::<name-with-"(shareable)"-stripped>`. Finds the vertically-centered section, posts `{t:'goto', i:index}` on scroll (debounced via rAF + 900ms suppress window to avoid echo loops), listens for peer messages and smooth-scrolls to match. No-ops silently if no peer window / no `BroadcastChannel` support. This is the same self-contained sync mechanism worth flagging as a reusable pattern for Torq's own instructor/student dual-deck setup.
- **Print stylesheet** — flattens scroll-snap/animations, forces white background + dark text, hides UI chrome (nav dots, progress bar, timers), one section per printed page.
- **Reduced-motion**: full `prefers-reduced-motion` block disables all transitions/animations and forces sections visible.
- No scoring/quiz logic in this artifact (that's likely in the Lab Guide / Strategy Sprint Builder, not yet captured).

What moving up takes

The behaviors a Torq lead can actually watch happen, the five capabilities that get earned separately — and why Tier 2 is a place to stay if you want to.

Course Before You Start Read time ~8 min

Task 2 placed you. This one is about what changes between tiers — stated as things someone can watch you do on an engagement, rather than as adjectives on a rubric. Start with the part most tier models get wrong.

Tier 2 is a destination, not a waiting room

Tier 2 is where most Torq client work actually gets done. A tier model that reads as "everyone is en route to Tier 3" misdescribes the majority of the population and quietly devalues the tier carrying the engagements.

A high-performing, independent Tier 2 consultant creates real value and is someone a firm is happy to keep indefinitely. Nobody is stalling by staying. If you read the rest of this task as a ladder you're obliged to climb, you've read it wrong — read it as a description of what changes, so you can decide whether you want that change.

Stated plainly: the step from Tier 1 to Tier 2 is a job change, not a skill upgrade. Getting better at producing artifacts does not get you to Tier 2. Getting better at owning ambiguity and defending a position does. That's why what follows is mostly defense rather than more execution craft.

Five currencies, earned separately

Product capability isn't one seniority score. Cracking the PM Career splits it into five things that are earned independently — which is a far more honest input to a staffing decision than a single number:

  1. Product skillsJudgment about what to build and why — the customer, the problem, the shape of the solution.
  2. Execution skillsGetting it shipped: specs, sequencing, unblocking, acceptance, delivery.
  3. Strategic skillsReading the market and the business, and connecting today's work to where it has to end up.
  4. Leadership skillsInfluence without authority — the defining Tier 2 skill, and the one most consulting engagements test hardest.
  5. People-management skillsHiring, coaching, running a team. Earned separately from every other row here.

A consultant can be strong on execution, still developing on strategy, and be genuinely good at their tier. That's a normal, staffable profile, and nothing to apologize for.

Two guardrails

Don't turn it into a rubric

Conventional ladders fail in one of two directions: too vague to act on ("demonstrates strong judgement"), or so specific they get applied inconsistently. Worse, they often just restate the scope someone was already assigned, which makes them circular — you're rated senior because you were given senior work. The tables above are deliberately behavioral and deliberately short, and expanding them into a scoring sheet would break them.

The consulting third rung is not people management

A standard PM ladder's third phase is organizational excellence — hiring, coaching, running the org. Only the advisor-to-executives half of that transfers to a Torq Principal advising a client team they don't manage. The manage-the-org half applies to an AD managing Torq consultants and to nobody else. Any tier model that borrows a standard PM ladder wholesale imports an assumption that doesn't hold here.

One level up:

Reading these behaviors across a whole team — who can be left alone with an ambiguous workstream, who needs the scope cut cleaner — is usually an AD/Director staffing call rather than yours. Knowing how the decision gets made is what lets you feed it good input now, and make it yourself later.

Tier 1 — a level deeper

Pick one row from the behaviors table — ambiguity is usually the highest-yield — and practice it on something small this week. Starting a piece of work before the question is fully answered, then bringing back options instead of the question, is the single most visible move on the list.

The move: one row, one deliverable, deliberately.

Tier 2 — the trade-off you can discuss

This is your tier and it may well stay your tier. The useful read isn't "what's missing" but "which currency is my engagement paying for" — a delivery-heavy engagement rewards execution and leadership, and will barely test strategy no matter how long you stay on it.

The move: if a currency isn't being tested by your staffing, it won't develop by itself — ask for the work that tests it.

Tier 3 — strategic & risk read

The currencies are a staffing and risk instrument. A team strong on execution and thin on strategic capability will deliver an engagement competently and miss the phase-two opening — which is a commercial outcome before it's a coaching note.

The move: staff the currency the engagement will test, and name the gap in the plan rather than hoping.

Key takeaways

  1. Tier 2 is a destination, not a waiting room. Most Torq client work happens there, and staying is a legitimate choice.
  2. Tier 1 to Tier 2 is a job change, not a skill upgrade. More execution craft doesn't get you there; owning ambiguity and defending a position does.
  3. Behaviors beat rubrics. Every row above is something someone can watch happen, and you can start doing it at your current scope.
  4. The five currencies are earned separately. Strong on execution and still developing on strategy is a normal, staffable profile.
  5. Don't expand this into a scoring sheet. Ladders that get specific enough to score end up circular — rating you senior for having been given senior work.
